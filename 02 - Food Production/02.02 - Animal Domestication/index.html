    Animal Domestication

    Animal domestication is the process by which humans selectively breed wild animals over generations to exhibit traits that are desirable for humans. This process typically involves controlling the breeding and living conditions of the animals to encourage certain behaviors or characteristics.

    Unlike taming, domestication predisposes an animal's future children to be friendly towards humans. This ensures that the species stays useful for generations to come.

    Invented: around 13,000 BCE1

    Uses - Labor - Food - Transportation - Clothing - Pest control - Companionship

    Domesticated Animals

    Humans have domesticated a wide variety of animals for various purposes. Here's a list of them organized neatly into a table:

    Animal Domestication Purpose
    Camels Transportation, milk, and meat
    Cats Companionship and pest control
    Cattle Milk, meat, and labor
    Chickens Eggs and meat
    Dogs Companionship, hunting, herding, and other tasks
    Donkeys Transportation and labor
    Ducks Eggs, meat, and pest control
    Geese Meat, feathers, and pest control
    Goats Milk, meat, and fiber
    Guinea pigs Meat and as pets
    Horses Riding, transportation, and labor
    Llamas/Alpacas Wool, meat, and pack animals
    Pigs Meat
    Rabbits Meat, fur, and as pets
    Reindeer Transportation, milk, and meat in Arctic regions
    Sheep Wool, meat, and milk
    Silk moths Silk production
    Turkeys Meat
    Water buffalo Milk, meat, and labor
    Yaks Milk, meat, and fiber in high-altitude regions

    Additionally, we've added images to show you what they generally look like. Note that some animals, such as dogs, vary quite considerably in appearance.
