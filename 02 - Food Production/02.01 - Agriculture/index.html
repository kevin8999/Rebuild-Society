    02.01 - Agriculture

    Agriculture is where you plant seeds into the ground and watch your food grow.

    You also no longer need to chase your food. Instead, you just watch the food grow yourself. Agriculture is the ultimate backbone of all civilization. Without it, we would've never developed an organized society.

    Getting Started

    Minimum requirements: - [ ] 2 - Stone Tools - [ ] Seeds - Seeds can be found in abandoned fields, gardens, barns, or seed vaults. - [ ] Legumes - enriches the soil - [ ] Open space with good soil to plant seeds

    Maize, wheat, and rice make up most of the crops that we grew and consumed. Others, such as corn, barley, oats, rye, millet, and sorghum, also make up a significant portion of the crops that we grow to eat (or feed to animals).

    These seeds are typically found in seed banks, but if none of those are around, you will need to look for them in the wild. Typically, wild cereal crops can be found in open areas (such as grasslands), near water sources, where herbivores (plant-eating animals) eat plants, or transition areas between different ecosystems.

    There are also seed vaults scattered around the world that preserve seeds for a very long time. However, some of these seed vaults may be very far away from you. We recommend only going after nearby places that may have seeds first. In case you would like to visit these faraway seed vaults, see [[Locations of Major Seed Vaults]].

    Farming

    Finding Suitable Ground

    The best land for farming includes: - Fertile soil - Suitable climate for crops you want to grow - Close proximity to a river or water source: used to water crops. - No contaminants that harm plants

    Finding good land ensures that your crops will grow well.

    Planting

    Before planting seeds, you need to make sure that they're ready for planting first. Usually, this means cleaning them.

    To plant your seeds: 1. Plow the soil. This turns over the uppermost soil, which brings fresh nutrients to the surface. 2. Spread the seeds out. 3. Rake the seeds evenly using a plow. 4. Water the seeds.

    To maintain your garden, make sure the soil is moist. You can check if the soil is moist enough by checking the top 3 centimeters of it.

    Crop Rotation

    Growing one single type of crop on one piece of land will quickly deplete the nutrients of the land. Therefore, a crop rotation schedule is needed to replenish the nutrients that that piece of land needs. Rotating your crops will allow you to make efficiently grow food while ensuring that your plants get all the nutrients they need in the future.

    The modern-day crop rotation system (the "Norfolk Four-Course System") requires you to use four separate fields and to plant in this order1:

    Field Number Year 1 Year 2 Year 3 Year 4
    1 Wheat Turnips Barley Clover
    2 Turnips Barley Clover Wheat
    3 Barley Clover Wheat Turnips
    4 Clover Wheat Turnips Barley

    However, if you don't have those crops, here is a more general table:

    Field Number Year 1 Year 2 Year 3 Year 4
    1 Cereal Crop Root Vegetable Animal Fodder Cover Crop
    2 Root Vegetable Animal Fodder Cover Crop Cereal Crop
    3 Animal Fodder Cover Crop Cereal Crop Root Vegetable
    4 Cover Crop Cereal Crop Root Vegetable Animal Fodder

    Each of these crops serve a specific purpose: 1. Cereal Crop: grown to feed humans. 2. Root Vegetable: grown to feed humans and to break up the soil. These crops grow in the ground and must be dug out when harvested. 3. Animal Fodder: grown to feed animals. 4. Cover Crop: grown to increase nutrients in soil.

    The efficiency of this practice can be further increased if you use animal manure (see 02.02 - Animal Domestication for more information on this).

    Agricultural Tools

    In this section, we go over the basic tools that you will need for farming. These tools make farming easier, and they have other uses too!

    Note that these tools shown are its modern versions. We doubt that people with just stone tools are able to craft complicated tools like these. A simpler version of these tools may be made as long as it accomplishes its goal.

    Scythe

    Uses: harvesting crops and mowing grass.

    To use the scythe, grab the handle ends (which are labeled as 8 in the image below 2). The bottom handle should be your back hand, and the top handle should be your front hand. Make sure the top handle is facing upwards. To cut crops down, just swing it at the desired height.

    The parts of a scythe correspond to the image's numbers and are listed below:

    1. Toe
    2. Chine
    3. Beard
    4. Heel
    5. Tang
    6. Ring
    7. Snath, snaith, or shaft: typically 170 centimeters long.
    8. Grips

    Note that the blade is about 60-90 centimeters long.

    This image shows you how to hold a scythe3:

    To use it, swing the scythe at a desired height. Note that you will need some practice to master it.

    Sickle

    Uses: harvesting crops.

    Sickles are one-handed tools that you carry to cut crops when they are ready.

    Modern sickles typically look like this4:

    Using this tool is straightforward. Just cut by moving it horizontally against the object you want to cut.

    Plow

    Plow (also spelled as "ploughs") turn over the uppermost layer of soil so that fresh nutrients are brought to the surface.
